# Appearance: minimalist adaptive theme (2026-11-07)

Presentation only. The JavaFX shell, navigation, feature panes, services, persistence formats, CLI, AI agent and Stage 1 artefacts are unchanged; the layout of every screen is the one already accepted. This document supersedes the wallpaper-based visual description in [user workspace](user-workspace.md#visual-design).

Git delivery (2026-11-08): the implementation histories below describe their original checks; their no-commit/no-push statements are superseded by the [authorized publication and verification record](../../docs/PROJECT_MEMORY.md#git-publication-and-verification-2026-11-08), including the intermittent Robot-click failure and passing targeted retry.

## What the user sees

- **Workspace chooser**: the macOS-style amber/blue wallpaper is replaced by an abstract wave background drawn in code. Workspaces are translucent cards (rounded-square avatar, name, soft shadow); the selected card is the more opaque one. Continue is a pill in the theme colour. Logo, brand, heading, arrows, keyboard (Left/Right/Enter), window controls and the selection flow are the existing ones.
- **Main application**: the same shell over the same wave background. Header, sidebar, panels and cards are translucent surfaces with a backdrop blur; selection, focus, primary buttons and accents follow the theme colour.
- **Preferences → Application Settings → Appearance**, in this order: Theme Color (five swatches, then **Custom Color** with a hue slider shown beneath them while it is selected), Appearance Mode (Light / Dark / Follow System), Transparency (Transparent ↔ Opaque slider, 0–100%), and the existing Language setting (same control and id). Every setting saves automatically; there is no Save button and no success message, and only a failed save shows a localized error (see [Automatic saving](#automatic-saving)).

Five low-saturation families, in this order: **Cool Blue** (default), **Sage Green**, **Warm Peach**, **Dusty Rose** and **Soft Teal**. Each defines a primary and a secondary hue in the same family, so changing the colour relights the background and highlights as well as the controls.

Refinement (2026-11-07, second pass): the first palette had Soft Blue and Lavender Purple, whose wave colours came from swapped secondary hues (Blue's waves were violet at 256°, Lavender's were blue at 228°), so each looked like the other. Lavender Purple was merged into **Cool Blue** (secondary hue now 212°, blue throughout; a saved `lavender` choice opens as Cool Blue) and **Soft Teal** (186°/196°) was added. Sage moved toward grey-green so it stays apart from Teal.

### Custom colour (2026-11-07, fourth pass)

A sixth swatch, set apart by a hairline, is the user-configurable **Custom Color** (not a sixth preset): a pastel spectrum disc whose centre shows the chosen colour. Selecting it shows a **Hue** slider beneath the swatches; its track is the pastel colour each hue produces, its thumb ring and the preview pill at its end use the live accent. Choosing a preset hides the slider; choosing Custom again restores the remembered hue.

The hue never reaches the interface at full strength. `Theme.Family.custom(hue)` generates a family like the hand-tuned presets: secondary hue 12° away for the waves, a per-hue chroma interpolated from the presets (greens and yellows read as more saturated, so they get less), a light swatch, and control colours whose saturation is moderate and whose brightness is solved for a target WCAG luminance — light-mode fill 0.165 under white text (≥ 4.6:1), accent text 0.085, dark-mode fill 0.37 under near-black text, dark accent text 0.50. Cyan becomes a gentle cyan, red a dusty rose; yellow becomes an olive fill in Light mode, because a light yellow fill could not carry white text. Every token, the wave lighting and the Dark relighting then derive from that family exactly as for a preset, so background, ambient highlights, selection, buttons, hover, focus and borders change together, and Dark is relit for the hue rather than inverted.

## Architecture

| Piece | Responsibility |
| --- | --- |
| `ui/gui/Theme.java` | Immutable record `(accent, mode, dark, opacity, customHue)` (`customHue` is `PRESET` for a preset). `family()` is the preset's hand-tuned `Family` or one generated from the custom hue; `tokens()` produces every semantic colour (`-pp-*` plus the modena base colours) from it for Light or Dark at the given surface opacity; `lighting()` gives the wave background's colours and light positions (`Lighting` of `Light` and `Wave` records, see [Wave background](#wave-background-2026-11-08-sixth-pass)). The single source of palette truth. |
| `ui/gui/WaveRenderer.java` | Procedural renderer of the flowing-wave background: three wave surfaces whose crests are cubic Bézier curves, radial light fields and gradients, evaluated per pixel from the distance to each curve, composited in OKLab and dithered against banding; plus a separable Gaussian blur with edge clamping. Pure computation, parallel by rows. |
| `ui/gui/ThemeManager.java` | Application-wide state and persistence. Generates the token sheet as a `data:text/css` stylesheet and places it directly after `photopilot.css` wherever that file is loaded (scenes and dialog panes, also tracked through `Window.getWindows()`), so a change restyles every open window in place. Follow System detection and polling. |
| `ui/gui/ThemeBackdrop.java` | Decorative layer added as the first, unmanaged, mouse-transparent child of the workspace, chooser and splash; shows the rendered field and holds the backdrop blur (below). Re-renders whenever the lighting, window size or display scale changes, coalesced to one render per frame; rapid changes (a hue drag) get a half-resolution draft and the full render follows 140 ms after the changes pause. The first paint is immediate; every later render runs on one background thread and is swapped in when ready (a superseded request is skipped). |
| `photopilot.css` | Every themed colour is a looked-up token. The `.root` block holds the Soft Blue / Light defaults; `ThemeTest` keeps them equal to `Theme`'s output. |
| `ApplicationSettingsPane.java` | The Appearance section; the language selector is the previous control, now saved silently. |
| `ui/gui/DropdownSkin.java` | Skin of every ComboBox (set by CSS `-fx-skin`): the standard skin plus the frosted backdrop behind the open list and placement inside the window ([Dropdowns](#dropdowns-2026-11-07-fifth-pass)). |

Dark mode is not a dimmed Light mode: the same composition is relit — a deep navy/charcoal base shared by every theme (the accent contributes only a trace), its own light positions, restrained coloured light instead of white highlights, charcoal-navy surfaces and pastel control fills with dark text. The first pass's scene-graph vector waves and their rim-light stroke (hard edges, an outline arc) are not used; the sixth pass draws Bézier geometry procedurally as gradients instead.

### Wave background (2026-11-08, sixth pass)

The earlier field was a diagonal gradient with wide, nearly straight bands and read as flat. It was redesigned as a layered, flowing-wave composition after six reference pictures the user supplied for **visual direction only**. No picture is in the repository, loaded, embedded, traced, sampled or filtered: the background is computed entirely from code and the current theme, and works with every reference deleted. `ThemeTest` checks that `WaveRenderer`, `ThemeBackdrop` and `Theme` load no resource or image. The JAR's only raster files are the existing logo PNGs and the two unused wallpapers listed under Limits.

- **Geometry** (`WaveRenderer.BACK`, `PRIMARY`, `SECONDARY`): each crest is a cubic Bézier curve in window fractions that starts and ends outside the window. The primary wave is a broad S-curve rising from the lower left (about 0.93 of the height) to the right edge (about 0.36); the secondary wave lies in front of it, nearer the bottom right; a faint back fold gives the upper area a little depth. The geometry is the same for every theme and mode; only colours and light change.
- **Each surface:** a soft occlusion shade just above the crest; a body that fades in below the crest (quintic smootherstep) and deepens with distance and towards the right; and a luminous rim along the crest. The rim is an asymmetric Gaussian band whose strength rises along the crest and varies gently, like light catching a fold. All three are functions of the distance to the curve measured along its normal (vertical distance × cos of the slope), so rims keep one width on steep and flat stretches. There are no outlines, strokes or polygons.
- **Layers, back to front:** a diagonal base gradient; a sky light (white in Light mode, top left); a side bloom in the family's companion hue; the ambient pool behind the content (unchanged from the third pass); the back fold, the primary and the secondary wave; then a glow in the companion hue over the lower right that lights both waves. Light fields are wide ellipses with a Gaussian falloff.
- **Palette generation** (`Theme.lighting()`, one generator for presets and custom hues): it uses the family's primary hue, its secondary hue and a **companion hue** `bloomHue(h) = h − 40·cos(h)`. That gives lavender beside blue, a blue beside teal, pink beside peach and mauve beside rose. It moves continuously with the hue, so a hue drag never jumps. The deepest Light-mode wave tones and every Dark-mode light and crest are **solved for a target luminance** (`Family.ofLuminance`), so a yellow and a blue hue get the same depth and glow, and text over any point keeps its contrast. Saturation is scaled by the family's chroma (pastel, never neon).
- **Light mode:** an airy near-white base, white sky light and white crest rims, pastel wave bodies. The deepest tones sit at a fixed luminance, so translucent panels over them keep body, muted and accent text at 4.5:1 or more (weakest measured 4.58:1, Soft Teal muted text at 0%).
- **Dark mode:** the shared navy/charcoal base; wave bodies of navy carrying part of the theme colour; crests lit in the theme colour and lights in the companion hue mixed with a little navy (so warm families get a plum light, never brown). Highlights stay restrained: the brightest point of any dark composition has luminance below 0.1.
- **Artifacts:** colours are composited in OKLab (perceptually even, no muddy midpoints between hues), converted back to sRGB exactly near black and through an interpolated table elsewhere, then dithered below one 8-bit step (triangular, deterministic). The steepest step between neighbouring pixels is at most 8.5 levels at the application's half-size render and halves when the resolution doubles: a gradient, never an edge, at any resolution.
- **Resolution and scale:** everything is in window fractions, with lengths in units of √(width × height / 1.6), so the composition keeps its proportions from 880 × 600 to a maximized 2560 × 1369 window. The field is rendered at half the window's **physical** size (×1.5 on a 150% display, at most 1600 px wide) and scaled smoothly; a move to a display with another scale re-renders it.
- **Performance:** the field and its blurred copy take about 7 ms for a 1120 × 760 window and 19 ms for 1920 × 1080 (24 threads), against 36 and 88 ms for the previous single-threaded field. Renders happen only when the theme, size or display scale changes, never on mouse movement, and off the JavaFX thread. During a 36-step live hue drag the slowest JavaFX round trip was 17–25 ms. There is no animation.

### Backdrop blur and transparency

JavaFX 21 has no backdrop filter (no `backdrop-filter` equivalent in its CSS or effects). `ThemeBackdrop` therefore renders a Gaussian-blurred copy of the background field once per window size or theme (at a quarter of the size, sigma 10 → about 40 px on screen, edges clamped so borders do not darken) and, after each layout pass, masks it to the visible bounds of the translucent surfaces (`app-header`, `navigation-scroll`, `surface-panel`, `status-tile`, `module-card`, `feedback-box`, `glass-panel`, chooser `profile-tile`), clipped by their scroll viewports. Dragging the slider only changes surface alpha (one coalesced stylesheet swap per pulse); it never re-renders the blur. At 100% the blur layer is hidden.

The slider sets **panel background alpha only** (`-pp-surface-*`, `-pp-card` tokens); panel nodes, text, icons and controls keep full node opacity. The mapping is close to linear: Light panels 12% → 34% → 56% → 78% → 100% at 0/25/50/75/100% (cards 22% → 100%), Dark panels 30% → 100%. Inputs and tables stay ≥ 84% opaque, buttons ≥ 52%, menus and dialogs are always opaque, and the actual window is never made transparent or click-through.

**Light-mode refinement (2026-11-07, third pass).** In Light mode a near-white panel over a near-white background looked the same at 0% and 100%: measured on the Settings panel, the background behind it was ≈ (225, 234, 248) and the 0% panel still kept 40% white, so the states differed by about 15 levels. No opaque parent or child layer was involved: the scroll viewport, content surface and rows are transparent and the blurred copy sits directly behind the panel. Changes:

- `WaveRenderer` adds an **ambient pool** of the theme colour (Light: saturation 0.17, strength 0.72, the same for every family; Dark: restrained navy-blended light) centred behind the content panels, with the same smooth falloff as the other light fields; the white corner glow was made smaller so it no longer washes that area out. Measured background saturation in the content area rose only modestly (Cool Blue ≈ 0.09 → 0.10–0.12; the top-left stays near white).
- **Glass treatment that recedes with opacity:** a bright white rim and a restrained top sheen (`-pp-glass-sheen`, layered on the panel background) while see-through, fading to a faint neutral hairline and no sheen at 100%; panel shadows are also lighter while see-through.
- The backdrop blur is slightly stronger (sigma 10 at quarter size, ≈ 40 px) so colour visibly diffuses behind the panel.
- Rendered result, averaged over a 12 × 6 grid across the Settings panel: Light 0% vs 100% differs by **26–28 levels** in every theme (steps 28 / 21 / 14 / 7 / 0), Dark by **14–16 levels** (14 / 11 / 7 / 3 / 0), monotonic at every step.

### Photographs

Nothing is applied to images: no effect, opacity or blend is set on any ancestor of an image view, the blur layer lies behind content, and image previews sit on an opaque neutral mat (`-pp-photo-mat`, white / #151515) that ignores the theme colour and Transparency. User avatar photos are drawn unchanged. Export, EXIF and image-processing code is untouched.

## Persistence

Saved in the existing `preferences.json` (same schema 1 string map as the language) under `appearance.accent` (`blue|sage|peach|rose|teal`, or `custom`; legacy `lavender` reads as `blue`), `appearance.mode` (`light|dark|system`), `appearance.opacity` (`0`–`100`) and `appearance.customHue` (`0`–`359`, kept while a preset is selected; missing or invalid → 262, out of range wraps). Files written before the custom colour existed open exactly as before. Two additive methods on `JsonLocalePreferenceRepository` — `values()` and `merge(Map)` — reuse its locking, atomic replacement and legacy-copy rules; `merge` refuses the `language` key so the language keeps its own validated path. Language saves keep appearance values and vice versa. Reading never writes; unknown or invalid values fall back to the defaults (Cool Blue, Follow System, 62%) without being overwritten; a malformed file is never overwritten and the change still applies for the session with a localized save error. Only the GUI's normal startup (`PhotoPilotGUI.start`) opens the file; tests and embedded panes keep appearance in memory.

### Dropdowns (2026-11-07, fifth pass)

Every ComboBox shares one design, applied centrally by `photopilot.css`, and no pane was edited for it. That covers the 22 declared in 13 feature and settings panes and the startup recovery dialog (status and type filters, session selectors, calculator, strategy, import, capability and Language choices), plus the lens choices the requirement editor creates per row.

- **Collapsed control:** the input treatment (radius 8, `-pp-input`, hairline border, focus ring while focused or open, stronger border on hover), its existing size, and a muted chevron that turns up in the accent colour while open.
- **Open list:** a level-2 surface (radius 12, 5 px inner padding, `-pp-surface-2` with the glass sheen, the receding rim `-pp-border` and a restrained shadow) with items of radius 7 and 6 × 10 px padding. Hover is `-pp-hover`; the selected item is the soft accent tint `-pp-selected-soft` with accent text instead of modena's solid selection bar, in every preset, the custom colour and both modes.
- **Transparency and blur:** the list's background uses the card token, so the slider maps it exactly like cards (Light 22% → 100%, Dark 40% → 100%) and changes apply to an open list. JavaFX popups are separate transparent windows and JavaFX has no backdrop filter, so `DropdownSkin` takes the window content under the list when it opens (24 px margin), reduces it to quarter size and blurs it with the same `WaveRenderer.blur` and sigma as the panels' backdrop, and shows it behind the translucent background, clipped to the list's rounded shape. It is hidden at 100%. Only the background is translucent: the list node keeps opacity 1 and the text and selection are drawn above the blur.
- **Placement:** directly under the control with a 4 px gap and the left edges aligned. If the room below is too small and smaller than above, the list opens upward. It always stays inside the window with an 8 px margin, and it is never taller than the room available, so long lists scroll (the visible row count still applies). It is as wide as the control, wider only when an option needs it, and never wider than the window, so options are never cut or scrolled sideways. JavaFX's own placement only flips by screen edges, not window edges.
- **Empty lists:** `FxLocalizer.choices` (which every choice list except Language already used) gives each ComboBox a localized placeholder, "No available options" / "暂无可选项", which shows as one short muted line instead of a blank panel. No option is added and loading is unchanged. The blank Planning selector was an empty data source (no sessions in that profile), not invisible text: with a session, its option renders normally.
- **Fonts:** the list uses the control's font, so Chinese options use the Chinese UI font that the window content uses.

A popup copies only its owner scene's stylesheets, so where `photopilot.css` sits on a dialog pane instead of the scene (the startup recovery dialog), the list carries the stylesheet itself.

`DropdownSkin` extends JavaFX's `ComboBoxListViewSkin` and is assigned with `-fx-skin`. Values, items, converters, cell factories, selection, filtering callbacks, keyboard (arrows, Enter, Escape), mouse, focus and accessibility are the standard skin's, and the open list is still its standard ListView.

### Automatic saving

There is no Save button. Swatches, Custom and the mode segments save on selection. The two sliders preview live and save once: a drag when it is released, keyboard or scroll steps 400 ms after the last step (`ApplicationSettingsPane.SAVE_DELAY`), so a slider never writes on every step. `ThemeManager` marks a previewed value as pending until it is saved; the page writes a pending value when it is discarded (for example when the workspace is rebuilt after a user switch) and `ThemeManager.shutdown()` writes it on normal application exit. The language still saves through `LocalizationService.selectLanguage` the moment it is chosen. A success shows nothing (the former "Language preference saved." message and the **Save preference** retry button are removed); a failure shows the existing localized error under the section, keeps the change for the session, never reports success, and the next change retries.

## Follow System

JavaFX 21 has no platform-appearance API (it arrived in JavaFX 22). The OS setting is read directly: Windows `HKCU\…\Themes\Personalize\AppsUseLightTheme` via `reg query`, macOS `defaults read -g AppleInterfaceStyle`, GNOME `gsettings … color-scheme`. It is read once at startup (≈60 ms on the development machine), whenever a PhotoPilot window regains focus (at most every 2 s) and every 15 s on a daemon thread while Follow System is selected. If it cannot be read, Light is used and Settings says so.

## Motion

No animations were added: appearance changes apply instantly, so reduced-motion preferences are respected by construction.

## Limits

- The main window keeps its native Windows title bar, which follows the OS, not the in-app Dark mode.
- The backdrop blur is an emulation (a pre-blurred copy of the background behind surfaces), so content moving behind another translucent surface is not blurred; only the decorative background is.
- While a window is being resized the field is stretched and re-rendered once resizing pauses (140 ms); after a theme change the background follows a few milliseconds after the panels, when its background render is ready.
- Follow System changes are picked up on focus or within 15 s, not instantly.
- `ProfileGuiTest` logs transient JavaFX "could not resolve"/conversion warnings for tokens while it rapidly rebuilds views (the baseline already logged the same kind for `-pp-text`; 18 in the fourth-pass run, up from 11, because the new switch test rebuilds the workspace three more times); final renders are correct and the real application's smoke run logs none.
- A dropdown's frosted backdrop is taken when the list opens (or the theme changes while it is open); content that changes behind an open list is not re-blurred until it is reopened. Text-field context menus (Undo/Copy/Paste) are not dropdowns and keep their previous look.
- Yellow custom hues give an olive accent in Light mode: a light yellow fill could not carry white text at 4.5:1, and the generator keeps contrast rather than hue lightness.
- The `.app-menu` rules, `AppIcons.CHEVRON_DOWN` and the `menu.switchWorkspace` translations served only the removed header menu; they are unused and were kept.
- `workspace-wallpaper.jpg`, `user-selection-wallpaper.jpg` (from the 2026-11-02 design; not the sixth-pass references) and `tools/BlurWorkspaceWallpaper.java` are no longer referenced; they were left in place and are still packaged in the JAR.
- Most of the waves sit behind the sidebar and content panels; at 62% they show through the panels frosted and fully in the open lower-right area. At the 880 × 600 minimum the panels cover most of the background.
- macOS/Linux rendering, display scales other than 100% and a forced 150%, and screen readers were not verified.
